Do I need a root canal?

Common signs, what the procedure actually does, and when it may be the best way to save a tooth.

8 min read · Reviewed August 2026

What a root canal actually treats

A root canal treats inflammation or infection inside a tooth. The goal is to remove damaged tissue, clean the inside of the tooth, and preserve the tooth when possible.

Common signs

  • Persistent tooth pain
  • Lingering sensitivity to hot or cold
  • Swelling near the tooth
  • Darkening or trauma history

Does every toothache need one?

No. Tooth pain can have several causes. An exam and imaging help determine whether the nerve is involved and whether another treatment is more appropriate.

What happens during treatment?

The tooth is numbed, an opening is made to access the internal tissue, and the canals are cleaned, shaped, disinfected, and sealed.

What happens after?

Many teeth need a final restoration such as a crown to protect them after root canal treatment, depending on tooth location and remaining structure.

When should I call a dentist?

Persistent pain, swelling, trauma, or worsening sensitivity should be evaluated. Severe swelling, fever, or difficulty breathing requires urgent medical attention.

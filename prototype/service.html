What implants solve

Replace the root and support a new restoration.

An implant is a biocompatible post placed in bone to support a crown, bridge, or denture.

The goal is not simply filling a space. It is restoring function while protecting the surrounding system where possible.

Important

Not every missing tooth needs an implant, and not every patient is an ideal implant candidate.

Stable function

Strong support for chewing when properly planned and maintained.

Natural appearance

The visible restoration is designed to blend with neighboring teeth.

Bone support

Implants can help preserve bone in the missing-tooth area.

Long-term planning

Treatment is planned around bite, health, and maintenance—not only the gap.

Who may be a candidate

The strongest plan considers the whole patient.

Implant success depends on more than whether there is an empty space.

  • ✓Bone volume and anatomy
  • ✓Gum and periodontal health
  • ✓Medical history and medications
  • ✓Smoking and healing risk
  • ✓Bite and restorative space

The process

What treatment can look like.

Exact timing varies, but the decision sequence is consistent.

  1. Consultation & imaging

    Evaluate the site, health history, goals, and whether preparation is needed.

  2. Treatment planning

    Organize timing, restoration design, and supporting procedures.

  3. Implant placement

    Place the implant and allow appropriate healing.

  4. Final restoration

    Connect the crown, bridge, or denture after healing and verification.

Common questions

Implant FAQ

How long does treatment take?

Timing varies based on healing, bone needs, and the type of final restoration.

Does implant placement hurt?

Local anesthesia is used during treatment; post-operative discomfort varies by case.

How long do implants last?

Longevity depends on health, hygiene, bite forces, maintenance, and other individual factors.

Will insurance cover implants?

Coverage varies by plan. The team can help review available benefits.
